Hyderabad sits on the Deccan plateau, which is mostly gentle and rocky rather than mountainous. That's good news for beginners: the easiest way to start trekking is with short, guided walks close to the city, then build up to a full day on a weekend trip, and only then try a multi-day trek. This guide sets out that path, with honest notes on how hard each step is.

## Step 1: Short guided walks on a camping trip

The gentlest start is a walk that comes with a night out.

- **Sunrise trek at Trance Veechika, Ananthagiri Hills.** A guided walk at 6 am through the Ananthagiri forest, included in the [Premium Camping](/trips/ananthagiri-trance-veechika-camping-from-hyderabad/) price.
- **Short trek at Harivillu, Ananthagiri Hills.** Part of the [Premium Adventure Camping](/trips/ananthagiri-harivillu-camping-from-hyderabad/) package, along with cycling and a rope course.
- **Trekking at Gandikota.** On the [Gandikota camping trip](/trips/gandikota-camping-from-hyderabad/), a professional trek leader takes the group on the canyon rocks on day 2. It's not long, but the rock is uneven and the drops are real, so good shoes matter.

## Step 2: A day climb near the city

**Bhongir Fort** (Bhuvanagiri) is on a large single rock about 50 km from Hyderabad, in Yadadri Bhuvanagiri district. The climb follows steps cut into the rock, with railings on steeper parts, and most people describe it as easy to moderate. Go early to avoid the midday heat, carry water, and check current entry rules and timings locally before you go. HCC doesn't run a Bhongir trip; it's a do-it-yourself day out.

## Step 3: A sunrise climb on a weekend trip

Once you're comfortable with a couple of hours on your feet, a weekend trip with an early climb is a good next step:

- **Mullayanagiri, Chikmagalur.** On the [Coorg & Chikmagalur](/trips/coorg-chikmagalur-from-hyderabad/) trip, the group starts at 5 am for Karnataka's highest peak (about 1,925 metres). The final stretch is on foot, up steps, and it can be cold and windy at the top.
- **Mandalpatti, Coorg.** A viewpoint walk on the same trip.
- **Vibhuthi falls, near Gokarna.** A short trek to the falls on the [Gokarna, Dandeli & Murudeshwar](/trips/gokarna-dandeli-from-hyderabad/) trip.

## Step 4: Your first multi-day trek

HCC's Sahyadri treks in Maharashtra run in the monsoon. Both are rated **moderate**, and both travel by overnight sleeper train to Pune.

- **[Andharban, Devkund Waterfall & Lohagad](/trips/andharban-devkund-lohagad-trek-from-hyderabad/):** Lohagad Fort on day 1, the Andharban forest trail on day 2 and the Devkund waterfall on day 3, with trek leaders and local guides. 5% GST applies.
- **[Harishchandragad, Kalu Waterfall & Aadrai Jungle](/trips/harishchandragad-kalu-trek-from-hyderabad/):** the Kalu waterfall trek, Harishchandragad and the Aadrai jungle trek over three days, with certified trek leaders, local guides and some meals included.

Monsoon trails are wet, muddy and slippery. They're beautiful, but they demand more care than the dry-season walks above.

## How to tell if you're ready

| You can… | You're ready for… |
| --- | --- |
| Walk 30 to 45 minutes on a path | Step 1: camp walks |
| Climb a few hundred steps with short breaks | Step 2: Bhongir Fort, Step 3: Mullayanagiri |
| Walk 8 to 10 km and handle uneven ground | Step 4: a moderate Sahyadri trek |

This is a rough guide, not a medical assessment. If you have a health condition, check with your doctor first, and tell the trek leader before you start.

## Beginner kit

- Trekking or sports shoes with grip, worn in before the trek
- A small backpack with 1 to 2 litres of water
- A light rain jacket (essential in the monsoon)
- Quick-dry clothes and a spare pair of socks
- Cap and sunscreen
- Personal medicines and a small first-aid kit
- A snack such as dry fruits or a chikki

More in [what to pack for a camping trip](/travel-guides/what-to-pack-for-a-camping-trip/).

## Trail habits

- Start early, and turn back if the weather turns.
- Stay with the group and the trek leader.
- Don't stand close to edges or enter water unless the leader says it's safe.
- Carry your rubbish back with you.
